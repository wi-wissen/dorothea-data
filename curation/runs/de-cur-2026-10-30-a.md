## Why

Since Dorothea PR #41 the sense ranking reads the preposition of a German verb (usage labels
such as `[with auf]` stored as sense frames, plus own cues for *kommen* + `von`/`auf`/`zu`).
The ranking only ever chooses among the **card senses**: `main` senses and uncurated ones. A
sense under `more` or `hide` never comes first, whatever the sentence. The earlier run had hidden
or demoted several prepositional senses, so for example *zu Geld kommen* still showed "to come".

## Method

For each verb: list every sense with its show/order, translations, German explanation, the
usage label of its source and the sense frame; look up own example sentences in both directions
(*Wir kamen zu keinem Ergebnis.*, *Was hältst du davon?*, …); then decide per prepositional use:
is there a sense, is it `main`, is the English clean, does it have a German explanation.

A quirk of the build shaped the records: in `de-de`, a later run's edit that names an English
Wiktionary sense id reaches a German Wiktionary sense only through the gloss anchor, and the
anchor only finds senses no earlier run curated. A later edit with a new `learner_de` on such a
sense would therefore add a duplicate in `de-de`. Senses that `de-de` holds under a German
Wiktionary id are thus changed without a new explanation, and three German-Wiktionary-only
senses are named by their own id (see "Unattached edits").

## Left for the engine

- **de-de, kommen zu:** the own cue looks for "arrive at", "obtain", "to come", "ankommen" in the
  card's English glosses or explanations; de-de cards carry no English, so *zu Geld kommen* still
  shows the "ankommen" sense first. A German cue word (e.g. *bekommen*, *erreichen*) before
  *ankommen* would fix it.
- **No frame for curated additions:** `missing` senses (*es geht um*, *zu etwas führen*) and
  senses carried into de-de have no sense frame, so the preposition cannot bring them forward
  (*In dem Film geht es um …* still shows "to go"). An own cue (*es geht um*) or frames for
  carried senses would.
- **Same frame on two senses:** *sorgen für* (look after / cause) and *hängen an* (hang / be
  attached) share the preposition; the object decides (*für Chaos*), which frames do not see.
- **gelten als:** needs a cue that separates *als* from *für*.
- **Lemma:** *gehört* in *Berlin gehört zu Deutschland* is read as *hören*; *Achte* at the start
  of a sentence as the adjective *achte*.
- **Measurement:** the held-out German set has no items for verbs with a preposition (issue
  #48); a test-set author could add some.
