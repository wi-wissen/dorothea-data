## Bulk instruction (verbatim)

# Run de-level-2026-10-20-a: CEFR levels for German headwords

You assign a CEFR level (A1, A2, B1, B2, C1, C2) to German dictionary headwords. The level answers:
**at which stage does a learner of German as a foreign or second language typically learn and need
this word?** Work from your own knowledge of German and of how German is learned. Do not look up or
reproduce any published word list (exam lists, textbook lists, vocabulary profiles), and do not
open any other files than the ones named below.

## Levels (own descriptions)

- **A1** – first weeks: greetings, numbers, family, food, the home, time, the most frequent verbs,
  adjectives and all core function words (articles, pronouns, common prepositions and conjunctions,
  question words, auxiliaries, modal verbs).
- **A2** – everyday routine: shopping, doctor, appointments, transport, work in simple terms, weather,
  feelings, describing people and past events; frequent concrete nouns and common verbs.
- **B1** – independent everyday use: opinions and reasons, work and training, authorities, media,
  health in more detail, travel problems, the news in simple terms; common abstract nouns.
- **B2** – abstract and specialised topics of general interest: society, politics, economy,
  environment, science in general terms; nuanced verbs and adjectives; formal letters.
- **C1** – precise, educated vocabulary for study and work: academic and administrative words,
  nuanced or less frequent synonyms, idiomatic and stylistically marked words.
- **C2** – rare, literary, technical, regional or old-fashioned words that even advanced learners
  meet only occasionally.

Guidance:
- Judge the word in its most common meaning (the English gloss shows which entry is meant).
- Compounds: the level of the whole word, usually not lower than its hardest part; transparent
  everyday compounds (Haustür, Kinderzimmer) can be as low as their parts.
- Specialist or school-subject terms (Photosynthese, Gleichung) are usually B2 or higher unless they
  are everyday words.
- Rude, vulgar or slang words: the level at which learners understand them, typically B2+.
- Use the whole scale. Do not default to B1 when unsure; decide, and mark `unsure` instead.
