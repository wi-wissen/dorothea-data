## Spot check

100 entries drawn at random (seed 7) from the 16,747 entries of run a. An agent that took no part in
run a judged each word from lemma, part of speech and gloss, first on its own and then compared with
the assigned level (instruction below, verbatim).

| Assigned | Items | Checker minus assigned (A1 = 1 … C2 = 6) |
|---|---:|---:|
| all | 100 | −0.20 |
| A1–A2 | 16 | +0.50 |
| B1–B2 | 37 | −0.08 |
| C1–C2 | 47 | −0.53 |

Verdicts: 95 acceptable, 3 too high, 2 too low (error rate 5 %). 98 of 100 within one level.
Patterns reported: the top end is about half a level too strict (concrete narrative words and
formal/news words pushed one step up); low ratings sometimes follow a word's common sense instead of
the sense shown by the gloss.

> You are an independent checker (you did not take part in assigning these levels). File /home/user/dorothea-build/levels-de/sample-100.tsv lists 100 randomly drawn German dictionary headwords with part of speech, an English gloss (shows which sense is meant) and the CEFR level (A1–C2) our pipeline assigned. Level meaning: at which stage a learner of German as a foreign/second language typically learns and needs the word (A1 first weeks; A2 everyday routine; B1 independent everyday use; B2 abstract topics of general interest; C1 precise educated/academic/administrative vocabulary; C2 rare, literary, technical, regional or old-fashioned).
>
> Judge each word yourself from your knowledge of how German is learned — first decide your own level WITHOUT looking at the assigned one if you can (e.g. write your levels first from lemma+pos+gloss, then compare), do not consult published lists. Write /home/user/dorothea-build/levels-de/sample-100-check.tsv with header `lemma	pos	assigned	checker	verdict` where verdict is `ok` (same or you'd accept it), `too_high` or `too_low` (only if you disagree by at least one full level and are fairly sure). Report back only: counts ok/too_high/too_low, mean signed difference (checker minus assigned, A1=1…C2=6) over all 100, the same split for assigned A1–A2 vs B1–B2 vs C1–C2, and 2–3 patterns in your own words.

## Correction

Only the C1 and C2 bands showed a systematic shift, so only they were corrected, and with an
independent signal instead of the sample itself: within each of the two levels, entries that are
not anchored by the own A1/A2 list and whose lemma is more frequent in running text than the
level's median move one level down (C2 → C1, C1 → B2). 3,455 entries changed.

| | A1 | A2 | B1 | B2 | C1 | C2 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| before | 1,008 | 1,896 | 2,296 | 4,751 | 4,726 | 2,190 |
| after | 1,008 | 1,896 | 2,296 | 7,111 | 3,461 | 1,095 |

Against the same 100 checker judgements: C1–C2 mean difference −0.53 → +0.04, exact agreement
overall 55 → 62. (The sample motivated the correction but did not choose the words; the frequency
rule did.)
