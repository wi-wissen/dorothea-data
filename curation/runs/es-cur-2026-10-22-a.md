## Instruction for the curators (verbatim)

# Run es-cur-2026-10-22-a: learner-ready Spanish entries

You curate Spanish dictionary entries for **Dorothea**, a reading aid for learners and school students
(German speakers learning Spanish, English speakers learning Spanish, and Spanish readers who want a
plain explanation). The sources are the Spanish, German and English Wiktionary (CC BY-SA). A reader
taps a Spanish word in a text and sees a small card: it must show the senses a reader is likely to
need, a short explanation in simple Spanish, and correct German and English translations. Work from
your own knowledge of Spanish, German and English. Do not consult other dictionaries or word lists,
and do not open any files other than the ones named below.

## Input

`/home/user/dorothea-build/cur-es1/batch-NN.jsonl`, one entry per line: `key, lemma, pos, rank`
(frequency rank of the lemma, lower = more frequent) and `senses`: `id`, `def` (Spanish definition,
possibly cut at 300 characters), `tags` (usage labels), `de` (German translations currently attached),
`en` (English translations currently attached). Some translations were attached automatically by
reversing translation tables (they may be wrong or belong to another sense). Obsolete, archaic and
similar senses were already removed.

## For every sense decide

1. **`show`**:
   - `main`: a sense a typical reader of everyday, school, news or fiction texts meets, including
     frequent spoken and colloquial uses (coloquial) and common news words. Card limit per **lemma**
     (all parts of speech of the same lemma together, also across batches): 6, up to 8 for very
     versatile words (hacer, tener, dar, poner, pasar, llevar, echar, quedar, ser, estar …).
   - `more`: correct and occasionally useful, but secondary. Shown under "Weitere Bedeutungen".
   - `hide`: very rare, regional-only, narrow technical or jargon senses, pure cross-references, and
     **duplicates**. Hide a sense as a duplicate only if it means the same as a kept sense; move its
     correct translations to the kept sense via `de_add`/`en_add`.
2. **`order`**: 1, 2, 3 … over `main` senses first, then `more` senses; most common first.
3. **`learner_es`** (for `main` and `more`): a short explanation in simple Spanish, ≤ 15 words, A2–B1
   vocabulary where possible. It must describe the **same sense** as the translations you keep. Do not
   use the headword or a word built from it; no idioms. Write it yourself; do not copy the Wiktionary
   wording. For function words, describe the use ("sirve para unir dos frases cuando …").
4. **German** (for `main` and `more`):
   - `de_keep`: given German translations that are correct for this sense (keep their spelling).
   - `de_drop`: only translations that are **wrong or misleading** for this sense. A correct second
     choice is never dropped. A string that packs several translations or contains explanations counts
     as misleading: drop it and add the clean single words.
   - `de_add`: 1–3 common German translations if the sense has none correct, or a clearly better
     everyday one is missing. Nouns with article (`das Haus`), verbs in the infinitive, separable verbs
     written together, base forms only.
5. **English** (for `main` and `more`): `en_keep`, `en_drop`, `en_add` with the same rules (verbs as
   `to …`, nouns without article).
6. **`missing`** (entry level, optional): a common meaning no sense covers, as
   `{"learner_es": "…", "de": ["…"], "en": ["…"]}`.
7. **`see`** (entry level, optional): if all senses are only cross-references, spelling variants or
   inflected forms, the base word they point to (e.g. `"see": "ir"`). Hide such senses.
8. **`note`** (optional, ≤ 10 words): why, if you hid a frequent-looking sense.

## Output

`/home/user/dorothea-build/cur-es1/out-NN.jsonl`, one line per input entry, same order:

```json
{"key": "es:casa:noun:1", "senses": [{"id": "…", "show": "main", "order": 1, "learner_es": "edificio donde vive la gente", "de_keep": ["Haus n"], "de_drop": [], "de_add": [], "en_keep": ["house"], "en_drop": [], "en_add": []}, {"id": "…", "show": "hide"}], "missing": null, "see": null}
```

Every input sense must appear exactly once with its `id`, in input order. Valid JSON per line. Write
every decision yourself (scripts only to assemble and validate). Keep working files in your own new
directory under /tmp/claude-0/. Verify with a short script before finishing: same keys and sense ids in
the same order as the input, allowed values only, card limit per lemma, `order` puts all `main` before
`more` and numbers shown senses 1..n, `learner_es` ≤ 15 words and not containing the headword,
`de_keep`/`de_drop`/`en_keep`/`en_drop` only contain strings given for that sense, no string both kept
and dropped.

## Lessons from the French run (follow them — a check found errors in 40 % of frequent entries there)

1. **Never hide the everyday meaning.** It may exist only as a sense without Spanish definition
   (translation-only rows from the German or English Wiktionary, ids `dewikt-…`/`en-…`). If that row is
   the best fit, show it and write `learner_es` yourself; otherwise put its translations on the best
   matching sense. A frequent word must never show only rare or literal senses.
2. **Card on the right sense id.** `learner_es` and the kept translations must match the definition of
   the sense they sit on; if not, move them to the matching sense.
3. **Frequent function-word uses** (relative *que*, *se* uses, *lo* + adjective, *por*/*para*) must be shown.
4. **Fixed expressions** that are the reason a reader taps the word (sin embargo, a pesar de, de
   repente, por supuesto, a menudo, en cuanto a, darse cuenta, tener que, hay que) go into `missing`
   when no sense covers them.
5. **Order:** the everyday sense first, never the dictionary's first sense by default.
6. **Do not drop correct second translations.** Only wrong or misleading strings go to `*_drop`.
7. **Packed or explanatory strings** ("issue, matter, topic", "to be necessary (that)"): drop them and
   add clean single words.
8. **No headword in `learner_es`**, not even in an example inside it ("la casa de …"), also not in
   `missing`; check short words (se, lo, la, de, en, que) especially.
9. **Format:** German nouns in `de_add` with article (`die Tugend`, never `Tugend f`); English without
   article; `*_add` must not repeat a kept string (not even with "to" or an article added).
10. Many German translations were added automatically through English ("pivot") or by reversing
    tables and may be wrong or sit on the wrong sense — check them like any other.

# Repair pass: hidden everyday meanings (run es-cur-2026-10-22-a, step 3)

Read BRIEF.md first; all its rules and the output format apply. A check of a sample found errors in
16 % of entries. The most frequent visible error: **an everyday meaning was hidden because it only
existed as a sense row without Spanish definition** (ids `dewikt-…` or `en-…`, translation-only rows),
or because the row's translation strings were messy (a packed or explanatory string, a wrong pivot).
Examples from the check: the language sense of a nationality noun (only "Spaniard" was shown); "follow
someone" for a verb of following; "set up / assemble" for a verb whose only shown sense was riding;
"be admitted (to hospital)"; "have available" (disponer de); "junto a". Also missed: fixed expressions
(hay que, en relación con, cada vez más, como máximo) and the core literal sense of a verb when the
definitions only cover abstract uses (serving food).

The lines in your file are all entries of lemmas where at least one hidden translation-only row carries
translations that no shown sense of that lemma has. For each lemma: look at every hidden row; if it
carries a meaning a reader of everyday, school or news texts needs and no shown sense covers it, show
it (main or more), write `learner_es`, clean its strings (drop packed/wrong ones, add clean words).
If the hidden row is a duplicate of a shown sense, keep it hidden but make sure the shown sense has the
correct translations. Add a missing fixed expression to `missing` when none is there and it matters.
Leave everything else as it is. Respect the card limit per lemma across all its entries in your file.

Output: for every entry you CHANGE, one line with the complete corrected output entry plus "why".
Validate with a short script (BRIEF rules). Write every decision yourself.
