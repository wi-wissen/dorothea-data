## Instruction for the bulk agents (verbatim)

# Run fr-cur-2026-10-24-a: learner-ready French entries

You curate French dictionary entries for **Dorothea**, a reading aid for learners and school students
(German speakers learning French, English speakers learning French, and French readers who want a
plain explanation). The sources are the French, German and English Wiktionary (CC BY-SA). A reader
taps a French word in a text and sees a small card: it must show the senses a reader is likely to
need, a short explanation in simple French, and correct German and English translations. Work from
your own knowledge of French, German and English. Do not consult other dictionaries or word lists,
and do not open any files other than the ones named below.

## Input

`/home/user/dorothea-build/cur-fr1/batch-NN.jsonl`, one entry per line: `key, lemma, pos, rank`
(frequency rank of the lemma, lower = more frequent) and `senses`: `id`, `def` (French definition,
possibly cut at 300 characters), `tags` (usage labels), `de` (German translations currently attached),
`en` (English translations currently attached). Some translations were attached automatically by
reversing translation tables (they may be wrong or belong to another sense). Obsolete, archaic and
similar senses were already removed.

## For every sense decide

1. **`show`**:
   - `main`: a sense a typical reader of everyday, school, news or fiction texts meets, including
     frequent spoken and colloquial uses (familier) and common news words. Card limit per **lemma**
     (all parts of speech of the same lemma together, also across batches): 6, up to 8 for very
     versatile words (faire, prendre, mettre, passer, tenir, tirer, aller, avoir, être …).
   - `more`: correct and occasionally useful, but secondary. Shown under "Weitere Bedeutungen".
   - `hide`: very rare, regional-only, narrow technical or jargon senses, pure cross-references, and
     **duplicates**. Hide a sense as a duplicate only if it means the same as a kept sense; move its
     correct translations to the kept sense via `de_add`/`en_add`.
2. **`order`**: 1, 2, 3 … over `main` senses first, then `more` senses; most common first.
3. **`learner_fr`** (for `main` and `more`): a short explanation in simple French, ≤ 15 words, A2–B1
   vocabulary where possible. It must describe the **same sense** as the translations you keep. Do not
   use the headword or a word built from it; no idioms. Write it yourself; do not copy the Wiktionary
   wording. For function words, describe the use ("sert à relier deux phrases quand …").
4. **German** (for `main` and `more`):
   - `de_keep`: given German translations that are correct for this sense (keep their spelling).
   - `de_drop`: only translations that are **wrong or misleading** for this sense. A correct second
     choice is never dropped. A string that packs several translations or contains explanations counts
     as misleading: drop it and add the clean single words.
   - `de_add`: 1–3 common German translations if the sense has none correct, or a clearly better
     everyday one is missing. Nouns with article (`das Haus`), verbs in the infinitive, separable verbs
     written together, base forms only.
5. **English** (for `main` and `more`): `en_keep`, `en_drop`, `en_add` with the same rules (verbs as
   `to …`, nouns without article).
6. **`missing`** (entry level, optional): a common meaning no sense covers, as
   `{"learner_fr": "…", "de": ["…"], "en": ["…"]}`.
7. **`see`** (entry level, optional): if all senses are only cross-references, spelling variants or
   inflected forms, the base word they point to (e.g. `"see": "aller"`). Hide such senses.
8. **`note`** (optional, ≤ 10 words): why, if you hid a frequent-looking sense.

## Output

```json
{"key": "fr:maison:noun:1", "senses": [{"id": "…", "show": "main", "order": 1, "learner_fr": "bâtiment où l'on habite", "de_keep": ["Haus n"], "de_drop": [], "de_add": [], "en_keep": ["house"], "en_drop": [], "en_add": []}, {"id": "…", "show": "hide"}], "missing": null, "see": null}
```

Every input sense must appear exactly once with its `id`, in input order. Valid JSON per line. Write
every decision yourself (scripts only to assemble and validate). Keep working files in your own new
directory under /tmp/claude-0/. Verify with a short script before finishing: same keys and sense ids in
the same order as the input, allowed values only, card limit per lemma, `order` puts all `main` before
`more` and numbers shown senses 1..n, `learner_fr` ≤ 15 words and not containing the headword,
`de_keep`/`de_drop`/`en_keep`/`en_drop` only contain strings given for that sense, no string both kept
and dropped.

## Instruction for the repair agents (verbatim)

# Repair pass: frequent French entries (run fr-cur-2026-10-24-a, step 3)

Read BRIEF.md first: it is the instruction the curators followed, and all its rules and the output
format still apply. You get entries of the 1,000 most frequent French lemmas, each line
`{batch, input, output}` (input = original entry, output = the curator's decision). An Opus check of a
sample found errors in about 40 % of the frequent entries. Fix the output so that a reader who taps
this word in an everyday text sees the right card. Typical errors to look for:

1. **Main meaning hidden.** The everyday meaning sometimes exists only as a sense without French
   definition (translation-only rows from the German or English Wiktionary, ids with `dewikt-` or
   `enwikt-`). Curators often hid it and kept a literal or rare sense. If the best fitting sense row
   for the everyday meaning is such a row, show it (write `learner_fr` yourself); otherwise put the
   translations on the best matching sense. Never leave a frequent word with only rare senses shown.
2. **Card on the wrong sense id.** learner_fr and translations agree with each other but sit on a
   sense whose definition means something else. Move them to the matching sense.
3. **Frequent function-word uses hidden or missing** (relative "que", pronoun "y", "pour" + infinitive).
4. **Fixed expressions not covered** (à cause de, d'après, quant à, avoir beau, bon marché, en effet):
   add as `missing` when no sense covers them and they are the reason a reader taps the word.
5. **Order:** the everyday sense first (troubler = disturb before "make cloudy"; temps = weather and
   time before verb tense).
6. **Correct second translations dropped.** The BRIEF allows dropping only wrong or misleading
   strings. Restore correct ones to `*_keep`.
7. **Packed or explanatory strings kept** ("issue, matter, topic"): drop and add clean single words.
8. **Headword in learner_fr**, including short words (se, y, ou) and inside `missing`.
9. **Format:** German nouns in `de_add` with article (`die Tugend`, not `Tugend f`); English nouns
   without article; `*_add` must not repeat kept strings.

Do not rewrite what is fine. Output: for every entry you CHANGE, one line with the complete corrected
output entry (same key, every sense id in input order) plus `"why"` (a few English words). Validate
with a short script against the BRIEF rules (ids and order, show values, order 1..n main before more,
keep/drop only given strings, nothing both kept and dropped, learner_fr ≤ 15 words without headword,
card limit 6 main per lemma, 8 for faire/prendre/mettre/passer/tenir/tirer/aller/avoir/être; the limit
applies per lemma across parts of speech, and all entries of a lemma are in your file). Write every
decision yourself; scripts only to assemble and validate.
