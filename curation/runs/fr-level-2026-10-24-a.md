## Instruction for the bulk agents (verbatim)

# Run fr-level-2026-10-24-a: CEFR levels for French headwords

You assign a CEFR level (A1, A2, B1, B2, C1, C2) to French dictionary headwords. The level answers:
**at which stage does a learner of French as a foreign language (for example a German-speaking school
student who learns French as a second foreign language) typically learn and need this word?** Work
from your own knowledge of French and of how French is learned. Do not look up or reproduce any
published word list (exam lists, textbook lists, référentiels, vocabulary profiles, FLELex), and do not
open any other files than the ones named below.

## Levels (own descriptions)

- **A1** – first weeks: greetings, numbers, family, food, the home, time, the most frequent verbs and
  adjectives, and all core function words (articles, pronouns, common prepositions and conjunctions,
  question words, être/avoir, common modal uses like pouvoir/vouloir/devoir).
- **A2** – everyday routine: shopping, doctor, appointments, transport, school, simple work, weather,
  feelings, describing people and past events; frequent concrete nouns and common verbs.
- **B1** – independent everyday use: opinions and reasons, work and training, media, health in more
  detail, travel problems, the news in simple terms; common abstract nouns.
- **B2** – abstract and specialised topics of general interest: society, politics, economy,
  environment, science in general terms; nuanced verbs and adjectives; formal letters.
- **C1** – precise, educated vocabulary for study and work: academic and administrative words, nuanced
  or less frequent synonyms, idiomatic and stylistically marked words (soutenu).
- **C2** – rare, literary, technical, regional or old-fashioned words (passé simple-only literary verbs,
  archaisms) that even advanced learners meet only occasionally.

Guidance:
- Rate the **word as learners meet it in its most common meaning** in this part of speech (the gloss —
  German, English or a French definition — shows which entry is meant). Do not rate a single rare sense
  that a gloss happens to show if the word itself is common.
- Derived words and compounds: the level of the whole word, usually not lower than its hardest part;
  transparent everyday words can be as low as their parts.
- Colloquial and slang words (familier, argot, verlan): the level at which learners understand them,
  typically B1–B2 for very common familier words (bouquin, boulot, mec), C1+ for argot.
- School-subject and specialist terms are usually B2 or higher unless they are everyday words.
- Use the whole scale. Do not default to B1 when unsure; decide, and mark `unsure` instead.
- `cefr` may be `-` when the row is not a word a learner needs a level for: a name filed as a common
  word, a fragment, an abbreviation that is not used as a word, a foreign word that is not French.

## Instruction for review 2 (verbatim)

# Second review pass: French CEFR levels (frequent words)

Read BRIEF.md in this directory first (scale and rules). A first review of a sample showed that the
first-pass ratings are systematically too strict for frequent words. Apply these calibration points:

- Rate the word as a learner meets it in its most common meaning, never by an odd first gloss
  (arriver, terminer, généralement, économie are A1–B1 whatever the gloss shows).
- Everyday news and civic vocabulary (tax, budget, court, municipal, law, election, sector) is B2,
  not C1. General formal verbs (procéder, émettre, affecter, insérer, différer) are B2.
- Frequent connectors and fixed phrases (auprès de, voire, à l'égard de, susceptible de, lors de) are
  B1–B2 according to how early learners need them.
- C1 = educated, literary-but-still-used or academic words; C2 only for truly rare, archaic, regional or
  narrowly technical words.
- Cardinal numbers up to 100 (including the quatre-vingt forms) are A1.
- Nationality adjectives and nouns of well-known countries: A2–B1; of less-known regions: B2–C1;
  inhabitant names of towns (brestois): C2.
- Transparent derivations sit at or one step above their base word.
- Also correct ratings that are too LOW (an academic term rated A2 because it looks short).
- freq_rank is one signal; ranks can be inflated by name or homograph collisions.

Do not consult published level lists (FLELex etc.). Judge row by row yourself; scripts only to validate.
Output: review2-K.out.tsv with header `id	lemma	pos	current	new	note`, one row per input row in input
order; note empty when unchanged, else a few English words.
