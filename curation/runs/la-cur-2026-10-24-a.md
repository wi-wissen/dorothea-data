## Instruction for the bulk agents (verbatim)

# Run la-cur-2026-10-24-a: learner-ready Latin entries

You curate Latin dictionary entries for **Dorothea**, a reading aid for school students who read
Latin (German speakers above all, English speakers too). A pupil taps a Latin word in a text and sees
a small card with the dictionary form and its meanings in German (direction la → de) or English
(la → en). The card must show the meanings a pupil reading school Latin (textbook texts, Caesar,
Cicero, Nepos, Ovid, Vergil, Catullus, Pliny, Seneca, the Vulgate) needs, most common first, with
correct German and English translations. The sources are the German and English Wiktionary
(CC BY-SA). Work from your own knowledge of Latin, German and English. Do not consult other
dictionaries or word lists (no Stowasser, Georges, Pons, Langenscheidt, Lewis & Short, school
vocabularies), and do not open any files other than the ones named below.

## Input

`/home/user/dorothea-build/cur-la1/batch-NN.jsonl`, one entry per line: `key, lemma, pos, rank`
(frequency rank, lower = more frequent), `head` (headword with vowel lengths), `forms` (principal
parts or genitive) and `senses`: `id`, `src` (`dewikt` = German Wiktionary, `enwikt` = English
Wiktionary), `def` (definition, German or English, possibly cut), `tags`, `de` (German translations
currently attached), `en` (English translations currently attached), `de_via_english: true` when the
German was attached automatically through English (often wrong: e.g. "to be" became "werden"),
`form_of` when the sense only points to another word.

Important: senses with ids starting `dewikt-` exist only in the German direction; senses with ids
starting `en-` exist in both directions. Many entries have the same meaning twice (once from each
Wiktionary).

## For every sense decide

1. **`show`**:
   - `main`: a meaning a pupil meets in school Latin. Card limit per **lemma** (all parts of speech
     of the same lemma together, also across batches): 6, up to 8 for very versatile words (sum,
     facio, ago, do, fero, habeo, res, ratio, gratia, peto, duco, teneo, cedo, ut, cum, quod, ad, in, ex).
   - `more`: correct and occasionally useful, but secondary (late, medieval, Christian or rare
     classical uses). Shown under "Weitere Bedeutungen".
   - `hide`: very rare or narrow senses, pure cross-references, and **duplicates**.
2. **Duplicates across the two Wiktionaries:** when a `dewikt-` sense and an `en-` sense mean the same,
   keep the `en-` sense (it exists in both directions), hide the `dewikt-` sense, and move its correct
   German translations to the `en-` sense with `de_add`. Keep a `dewikt-` sense only for a meaning no
   `en-` sense has.
3. **`order`**: 1, 2, 3 … over `main` senses first, then `more`; the meaning most common in school
   Latin first (never the dictionary's first sense by default: `peto` "to attack, make for" and
   "to ask for" both early; `consulo` "to consult" before rarer uses; `res` "thing, matter" first).
4. **German** (for `main` and `more`): `de_keep` (correct translations as given), `de_drop` (only
   wrong or misleading strings; a correct second choice is never dropped; packed strings with
   explanations like "räumlich (Richtung): in" are dropped and replaced by clean words), `de_add`
   (1–3 common German translations if the sense has none correct or a clearly better school
   translation is missing). German nouns with article (`das Haus`), verbs in the infinitive.
   Check every `de_via_english` translation especially.
5. **English** (for `main` and `more`): `en_keep`, `en_drop`, `en_add` with the same rules (verbs as
   `to …`, nouns without article).
6. **Case government** is part of the meaning in Latin: for prepositions and for verbs or adjectives
   whose meaning depends on the case they govern, a translation may carry a short marker in
   parentheses: German `(+ Akk.)`, `(+ Abl.)`, `(+ Dat.)`, `(+ Gen.)`; English `(+ acc.)`, `(+ abl.)`,
   `(+ dat.)`, `(+ gen.)`. Example: `in` → main senses "in, an, auf (+ Abl.)" as `de_add`
   ["in (+ Abl.)", "auf (+ Abl.)"] and "in … hinein, nach (+ Akk.)"; `consulo` "befragen (+ Akk.)" and
   "sorgen für (+ Dat.)". Use markers only where they change the meaning.
7. **`missing`** (entry level, optional): a list of common school meanings no sense covers, each
   `{"de": ["…"], "en": ["…"]}` (e.g. a fixed use like `gratias agere` "danken", `res publica`
   "der Staat", `memoria tenere`, `consilium capere`, `in animo habere`).
8. **`see`** (entry level, optional): if all senses are only cross-references, spelling variants or
   inflected forms, the base word they point to (e.g. `"see": "sum"`). Hide such senses.
9. **`note`** (optional, ≤ 10 words): why, if you hid a frequent-looking sense.

## Lessons from the French and Spanish runs (follow them)

1. **Never hide the everyday meaning.** A frequent word must never show only rare, late or literal
   senses; if the right meaning sits on a weak sense, show that sense and fix its translations.
2. **Translations on the right sense id**: kept translations must match the definition of the sense
   they sit on; move them otherwise.
3. **Frequent function words** (cum, ut, quod, qui, quam, ne, si, et, -que, atque, sed, enim, nam,
   autem, igitur): show every school use (`cum` + subjunctive "als, weil, obwohl"; `ut` + indicative
   "wie", + subjunctive "dass, damit, sodass"; `quam` "als" after a comparative, "wie" in
   exclamations, `quam` + superlative "möglichst").
4. **Order:** the school meaning first.
5. **Do not drop correct second translations.**
6. **Packed or explanatory strings**: drop and add clean words.
7. **Format:** German nouns in `de_add` with article; English without article; `*_add` must not
   repeat a kept string.
8. Translations attached through English (`de_via_english`) are often wrong or too narrow; check
   each.
